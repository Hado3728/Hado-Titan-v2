A Little Backstory About This Project

So, there's actually a bit more to this project than just the last few days. I'd pitched a version of this bot on Forge before, but it got rejected because I didn't have enough experience for the project. Honestly, that hit me pretty hard. I'd been really motivated to build this thing, and I'd even worked on the project during my midsems because I was so excited about it. Getting rejected after putting in that effort was pretty demotivating. But looking back, I can also understand why they said that. My design was a bit vague back then, and I probably hadn't thought through everything as much as I should have. Instead of completely giving up on the idea, I decided to take the feedback as a reason to improve the project. Since then, I've worked on my CAD skills and spent more time figuring out the actual engineering behind the bot. I also have some experience with electronics from before, although a lot of that work isn't on GitHub because I didn't really know what GitHub was until a year or two ago. And honestly, between competitive exams and everything else going on, I haven't had much time to work on the things I'm genuinely interested in. A lot of the time, I've just had to focus on studying and getting through exams.

Now that I'm in my first year of college, I really want to make the most of the opportunities I have. I'm already 18, and this is the last year I can make use of the opportunities at Hack Club. I don't want to let that time go by without actually building something I'm excited about. I want to be able to look back years from now and remember that I built a battlebot during my first year of college, even if the process was difficult. I know I still lack experience in some areas, and I'm not pretending that I know everything about building a battlebot. There's obviously a lot I'm going to have to learn along the way. But isn't that kind of the point of Hack Club? To build things, make mistakes, learn from them, and get better in the process? I don't want this project to be something I just talk about doing. I've already spent days working on the design, figuring out the components, and trying to make the fabrication practical. I want to take it further and actually build the thing. I know the previous rejection happened for a reason, and I'm not expecting this pitch to be accepted just because I've worked hard on it. I just hope the improvements I've made and the effort I've put into the project show that I'm serious about building it.

Hopefully, this time I get the chance to see it through.

JOURNAL

17th October

So I started working on this project again on the night of the 17th. I'd actually had this idea of building a battlebot about 1.5 months ago, and I was pretty inspired by the Nitro BattleBot at that time. I even went out of my way to contact the owner of the bot, Mr. Andrew, to ask him for some advice on building one. Thankfully, he actually replied!
His main advice was to focus more on the internal structure and making the weapon strong rather than just trying to maximise its energy. He also mentioned how reliability and durability are really important for a successful bot. Honestly, that was some pretty useful advice, and I wanted to keep it in mind while designing mine. At this point, I was basically revisiting an idea I'd had before, but this time I actually wanted to take it all the way to fabrication instead of just leaving it as an idea.

18th October

Today I started sketching out the bot in my notebook. I was actually on a family vacation at a hill station, but I still wanted to get some work done, so I took my notebook along and started roughly figuring out how I wanted the bot to look and how everything would fit together. The sketches were pretty rough, and I'm honestly not confident enough to post them here. But they helped me get the basic idea out of my head and onto paper before moving to CAD.

20th–26th October — Finally Starting the CAD

Okay, this is where I actually started building the bot instead of just thinking about it. I started working on the CAD model, and OH MY GOD, it was hectic. Getting all the components to fit together while keeping the design practical was a lot harder than I expected. I had to keep going back and forth between different parts of the design, making adjustments and figuring out how to model everything properly. I spent almost six full days getting the CAD to a stage where I was happy with it. I also used Claude to help with some of the modelling so I could speed things up a bit. It helped me work through certain parts of the process, but I still had to make sure the overall design worked for the bot I was trying to build. This part honestly took way longer than I thought it would. There's a massive difference between imagining a battlebot and actually trying to fit everything into a model where the components have to work together.

27th October — Almost Done With the Design

By 27th, I was pretty much done with the CAD, at least to the point where I could start preparing the design for fabrication. Before moving forward, though, I still had to figure out the battery calculations and the gear systems for both the drive motors and the weapon motor. I didn't want to just finish the CAD and immediately jump into fabrication without checking these things first. There was still a lot to consider, especially because the bot needed to be practical to build and not just look good in a CAD model.

28th October — Cost Cutting and Finalising Things

Today I pitched the bot to my dad, who's a mechanical engineer, to get his opinion on the design. I wanted him to look at it and suggest ways I could reduce the cost of fabricating the metal components. After discussing it with him, I went back and modified quite a few parts of the chassis. One of the main things I worked on was simplifying the design so that more of the components could be laser cut instead of requiring CNC machining. This was important because I didn't want the fabrication costs to get unnecessarily high just because I'd made the design more complicated than it needed to be. I also finalised the BOM (Bill of Materials) and started setting up the project repository on my laptop. I wanted to get the documentation and files organised instead of having everything scattered around in different folders.

29th October — Uploading Everything!

And finally I'm getting everything ready to upload to the repository and pitch the project on Slack. It's been quite a journey getting from the initial idea to an actual CAD model with the design and components planned out. I know there's still a lot left to do, especially once fabrication starts, but I'm really hoping this goes through. I've put quite a bit of work into getting the design to this stage, and I genuinely want to see this thing come to life. Now I'm just hoping the pitch gets accepted so I can move forward with the build.

Fingers crossed
